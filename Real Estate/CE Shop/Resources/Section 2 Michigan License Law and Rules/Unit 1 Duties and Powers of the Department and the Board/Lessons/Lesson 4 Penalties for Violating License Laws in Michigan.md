## 5. Misdemeanor Violations

The short path to a misdemeanor violation in Michigan is to practice real estate without a license. This includes the activities of a licensee or a broker, as well as anyone who operates a real estate education school. The penalties for misdemeanors can be steep, so it’s important to make sure you are fully licensed for the activities in which you intend to engage.

## 7. Recent Legal Changes

On July 25, 2024, new changes to the Occupational Code took effect. One of these concerns right-to-list home sale agreements. Under such an agreement, a residential property owner agrees to list their property with a broker at a future date in exchange for consideration.

Prohibited conduct for licensees now includes entering into a right-to-list sales agreement that is void and unenforceable for any of the following reasons listed under §339.2512g:

- The agreement is not in writing.
- The agreement has not been signed by every person with an ownership interest in the real estate.
- The agreement is for longer than two years.
- The agreement is missing one or both of the following required elements:

- An option for the owner to terminate the agreement before it expires, in which case the broker will be paid an amount that does not exceed the initial consideration the broker paid to the owner, with 6% annual interest accruing from the date when the initial consideration was paid until the date when the agreement is terminated
- An explanation of the early termination option, conspicuously displayed on the first page of the agreement, along with the period of time the agreement is in effect

On March 20, 2025, it became illegal to knowingly and willfully draft a fraudulent conveyance or submit it to a register of deeds with intent to defraud the property owner. Doing so is a felony punishable by up to 10 years in prison, a fine of up to $5,000, or both. A register of deeds who believes a conveyance is fraudulent may provide evidence of the violation to the local county prosecutor.
