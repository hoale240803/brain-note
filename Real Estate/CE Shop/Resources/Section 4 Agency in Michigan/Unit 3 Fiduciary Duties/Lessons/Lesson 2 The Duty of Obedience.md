
## Obedience

"It’s me again, Keira Levy. If you remember when I discussed the fiduciary duties of an agent, the O in OLD CAR stands for obedience. As your agent, I must carry out your instructions. I will give you my counsel, of course, based on my experience as a real estate professional, but your decision goes. "Let's spend this lesson getting under the hood of the duty of obedience."

An agent must carry out a client’s instructions. An agent will give counsel, of course, based on the agent’s experience as a real estate professional, but the client’s decision goes. If, for instance, a client says not to place a lockbox on the property, the agent must obey that instruction, even if it will cut down on showings. The agent should tell the client that they believe not having a lockbox will reduce showings and should perhaps offer some options to address the client’s concern, such as keeping the lockbox inside the house and only setting it out when showings are scheduled, which may resolve some of the client’s concerns. But in the final analysis, it’s the client’s home and the client’s decision.

It doesn't happen often, but sometimes the duty of obedience conflicts with the agent’s duty to treat all parties honestly and fairly. This might happen, for instance, if a client tells an agent not to respond to an offer. In that case, the agent will discuss the situation with the client, the ramifications, and then work to reach a resolution together. If, on the other hand, a client instructs an agent to do something that is unlawful, the agent can’t obey that instruction. If not resolved, the agent must terminate the agency agreement. Real estate agents must always abide by the rules and regulations of the profession and by federal, state and local laws.
