**The Duty of Confidentiality**

An agent can’t share any information the agent’s client asks to be kept confidential. This can get a little complicated when the information is a material fact relating to the property. Let’s say a client told an agent that a highway was going to be built behind the house but doesn’t want the agent to mention it to any buyers. This information materially relates to either the condition or value of the property. The agent must either convince the seller that this should be shared with buyers or the agent shouldn’t take the listing. See how the lines of confidentiality, disclosure, and obedience may sometimes conflict?

Example

Thomas, a seller, told Tina, his agent, that he really needed to sell his property because his relocation allowance was about to run out, and he would soon have to pay out of pocket for the current mortgage while living in another state. Thomas still wanted to get top dollar, however, and wanted Tina to keep his situation confidential. Even though Tina knew Thomas needed a quick sale, she couldn’t divulge her client’s situation because it’s confidential information, and it would harm Thomas’s negotiating position if it were divulged. If Thomas called Tina the following week and said, “Go ahead and say ‘price flexible,’” Tina could share that information with other agents and prospective buyers.

## Confidentiality Survives Agency Termination

The duty of confidentiality survives closing. This means that even after the transaction is complete or the agency relationship ends for any reason, the agent can’t divulge to any party, except by court order, confidential information shared by the client. The exceptions to this are:

- The information is known/made public from sources other than the agent
- Disclosure is necessary for the agent to defend herself against an accusation of fraud, or
- The former client is found guilty of fraud.

Note: Buyer name and addresses shall not be disclosed.

## Practicing Confidentiality

Let's see how well you exhibit the duty of confidentiality.

You represent Dennis and Candy. They got married a few months ago and want to sell the homes they each owned individually and purchase something new together. They want you to represent them in the sale of both homes and the purchase of a new home. That means you have not one, not two, but THREE commissions on the line!

Let's start with Candy's house. It's located on a quiet street with homes lining just one side. You ask her if there's ever been anything on the other side of the street. She says it's always been an empty field, but the city will be building a new park there soon. 

What do you do with this information?

Keep it confidential along with anything else Candy tells you.
==🟢Advise Candy that the park is a material fact that must be disclosed.==
Include in the property's listing information that there's a park across the street.
Ask Candy if you can share this information with buyers.

Great choice! This information relates directly to the property and could influence its value, so it's something that should be shared if it can be confirmed. Next, let's talk about Dennis's place. Dennis enjoys playing pool and didn't eat at home very often during his bachelor days so he thought, why not?
