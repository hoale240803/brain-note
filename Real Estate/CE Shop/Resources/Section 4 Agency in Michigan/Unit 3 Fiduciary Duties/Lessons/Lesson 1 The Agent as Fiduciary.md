## The Agent as Fiduciary

A fiduciary is someone placed in a position of trust for another. An agent is someone who acts on behalf of another. Agents must exercise all due diligence in fulfilling their clients' needs as much as possible—no shortcuts or "passing the buck" allowed. Here's how an agent might explain the duties he owes a client:

"I owe you **obedience**. So whatever you ask of me, if it's within my scope as your agent, and it doesn't break the law, I must do it. If you give me instructions that I believe are contrary to your best interests, I will tell you, but the decision is always yours.

"I owe you undivided **loyalty**. That means I can't put my or anyone else's interests ahead of yours when I represent you.

"I owe you full **disclosure**. That means I must tell you not only any facts I know about the property, but any material facts period. So if I know that a piece of land that you're selling would be worth more if it were subdivided into smaller lots, and each of those lots sold individually, I need to tell you that. If I know that a buyer is famous for lowball offers, I must tell you that. If I know that a similar house down the street is about to come on the market, I must tell you that. And if a buyer makes an offer on your property, and the buyer happens to be my brother, I must tell you that.

"I owe you the duty of **confidentiality**. This means that if you tell me something, provided it's not a material fact that I'm required to disclose, such as the presence of termites or the fact that the roof leaks, I have to keep it confidential. I can't divulge your confidential information to any prospective buyers or their agents, colleagues, or anyone else except under court order. So if you want to list your house for $300,000 but tell me in confidence that you really only need to get $280,000, and another agent calls me and says, 'I know you're at $300,000, but what will your seller take?' I have to answer, 'The seller's list price is as stated: $300,000.' This duty of confidentiality survives the end of our working relationship. It lasts forever.

"I owe you the duty of **accounting** for any funds or property I'm entrusted with on your behalf. This includes paperwork and money, and, like confidentiality, this duty survives the end of our relationship; if anything remains outstanding after the closing or termination of our agency relationship, such as paperwork or property, I must account for it to you.

"I owe you **reasonable skill and care**. This goes to my level of professionalism, training, experience, and work ethic. You can expect top-notch service from me. This means that I won’t look the other way if I see an opportunity or risk for you based on my experience. I will speak up. And I will continue to serve your interests throughout the transaction. It also means if something arises that is outside the scope of my expertise or licensure—perhaps a legal or tax matter—I will advise you to seek expert assistance."

## Is Keira Breaching a Duty?

Keira's sellers have decided to list their home at $300,000. They say they don't have much wiggle room on the price and ask that she not even bother with an offer less than $290,000. After a few weeks on the market, an offer comes in at $280,000. Her sellers are out of town and unreachable, and they won't be back until after the offer expires. Keira thinks for a minute about what to do, then calls the buyer's agent back to tell him that his clients need to bring the offer up; the listing price is $300,000.
