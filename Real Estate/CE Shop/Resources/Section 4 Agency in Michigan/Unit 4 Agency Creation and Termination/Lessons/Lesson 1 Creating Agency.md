
## The Creation of Agency

Agency may be created in several ways. Legally, it’s always best to create agency with intention and disclosure (and get it in writing). However, it’s also possible to create agency by your actions. (Keep in mind that some of the types of agency creation covered in this lesson are not allowed in all states.)

You can create an agency relationship through an **express agreement** or an **implied agreement**.

## Express Agency and Implied Agency

With **express agency**, you and your client agree to and understand the relationship. Express agreements may be written or unwritten, but written express agreements are ideal. They establish an agency relationship because they spell out the duties and responsibilities for both you (the agent) and your client. This allows you and your client to understand the expectations required for each party.

You and your client may enter into an agency agreement (aka listing contract or buyer representation agreement). This is also known as an employment agreement.

An agency relationship may be created without anything in writing, or even without verbal agreement. This is called **implied agency,** which exists when you act on behalf of a consumer in such a way that the consumer believes you are her agent.

Example

You’re representing a seller, and a potential buyer wants to make an offer on your seller’s listing. You assist the buyer by presenting the buyer’s offer to your seller client. During prior discussions with the buyer, you fail to disclose that you represent the seller; therefore, the buyer assumes you’re negotiating the offer on his behalf.

In this example, it could be implied that you’re acting as an undisclosed dual agent. As a result, you may be subject to disciplinary action and could lose the brokerage fee, or the transaction may be withdrawn.

Note: Even though implied agency can exist, you must establish agency in writing.

## Reduce Your Risk

Because of your responsibility to provide fiduciary duties to your client, either express or implied, you must be very careful with your actions. If you create an implied agency relationship, and it’s found that you didn’t provide fiduciary duties, you can be held liable for any damages incurred.

Follow these guidelines to reduce your risks of implied agency and undisclosed dual agency:

- Make sure your firm’s policies on agency and dual agency are clear, including the disclosure requirements.
- Clearly understand and communicate fiduciary duties as an agent.
- Before acting in any way as an agent, secure an express agency agreement in writing.
- If you’re engaging in dual agency, ensure you have all parties’ written consent.
- Provide all parties with the proper disclosures.
