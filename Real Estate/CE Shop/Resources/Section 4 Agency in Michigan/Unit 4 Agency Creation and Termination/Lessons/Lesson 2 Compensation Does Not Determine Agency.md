## Agency and Compensation Agreements

**Compensation Does Not Determine Agency**

It’s entirely possible that in a given transaction, you’ll be compensated for your brokerage services by a party other than your client. This might happen when, acting as a buyer’s agent, your brokerage may be paid a share of the compensation by the brokerage representing the seller or as a concession from the seller. Your brokerage passes on your share of the compensation to you. In such a case, you’re paid (indirectly) by the seller, but the seller isn’t your client, and you’re not the seller’s agent.

**Note:** Following the NAR settlement, buyer agent compensation may no longer be advertised on the MLS. Compensation is negotiated directly between the parties and documented in the buyer representation agreement and purchase contract. The core principle—that compensation does not determine agency—remains unchanged.

That’s straightforward enough. Let’s say, however, that you represent a buyer, and the seller is not offering buyer agent compensation, so the buyer’s father offers to pay your compensation. Who’s your client in this case? It’s still the buyer, not the buyer’s father. You don’t have an agency relationship with the buyer’s father. You owe your duties and loyalty only to your client, the buyer.
