## The Planning Board

A planning board is responsible for guiding the orderly growth and development of a community. Its primary function is to determine how land within the community will be used by establishing zoning districts and specifying permitted uses within those districts. These uses may include residential, commercial, industrial, single-family, and multi-family development. Planning boards may also review and approve certain special use permit requests. Requests for variances or exceptions to existing zoning regulations are typically handled by a zoning board of appeals.

The overarching goal of a planning board is to promote organized, sustainable growth rather than uncoordinated or inefficient development. Effective planning seeks to balance economic development with the overall well-being of community residents. Planning efforts generally focus on maintaining a high quality of life through amenities such as parks, schools, open spaces, and environmental protections, while also supporting livability by encouraging housing affordability and economic opportunity.

## The Comprehensive Master Plan

The foundation of community planning is the development of a **comprehensive master plan**. This long-range planning document provides an inventory and analysis of the community's characteristics, including its population, demographics, businesses, industries, physical infrastructure, and economic assets. The comprehensive master plan serves as a framework for future growth and development decisions by identifying community needs, resources, and priorities.

A comprehensive master plan also incorporates capital budgeting, which involves planning and funding long-term public projects and improvements. Capital budgets may include expenditures for facilities and infrastructure such as parks, recreation centers, swimming pools, amphitheaters, roads, utilities, and other community amenities. These budgets typically establish projected costs, funding sources, and implementation timelines to ensure that future growth can be supported by adequate public services and facilities.

## Public Input and Zoning Ordinances

Before a comprehensive master plan is adopted, planning boards often solicit public input through community meetings, public hearings, surveys, and other engagement methods. After conducting research and evaluating community needs, the planning board may submit recommendations to the local governing body, such as a city council, for review and adoption. Public participation helps ensure that planning decisions reflect community goals and priorities.

**Zoning Ordinances**

Once a comprehensive master plan is adopted, zoning ordinances are developed to implement the plan's objectives. Zoning ordinances regulate land use and development within the community and establish standards for factors such as permitted uses, lot sizes, building setbacks, structure heights, density requirements, and other development criteria. To administer these regulations, communities create zoning maps that divide land into designated zoning districts, such as residential, commercial, and industrial areas. Together, zoning ordinances and zoning maps provide the framework for directing land use and development in accordance with the community's long-term planning goals.
