## Other Federal Environmental Laws

Environmental regulations help protect natural resources and quality of life, but they also impact developers. It’s important for you to understand the environmental regulations that exist on the local, state, and federal levels that may impact development and livability in your market.

Four federal laws will be covered in this lesson:

- Clean Air Act (CAA)
- Clean Water Act (CWA)
- Resource Conservation and Recovery Act (RCRA)
- Toxic Substances Controls Act (TSCA)
## Clean Air Act (CAA)

The **Clean Air Act** was created in 1970 to authorize the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) to establish air quality standards for the protection of human health and the environment. It sets national air quality standards, regulates emissions from stationary and mobile sources, and authorizes EPA to control pollutants, including greenhouse gases

**How This May Affect Licensees**

For instance, due to EPA requirements established in 2010, Freon® (R-22) is no longer used as a coolant in air conditioners, refrigerators, and other cooling appliances. This legislation impacts both residential and commercial properties.

If a client is obtaining a home warranty for a property containing cooling appliances built before 2010, your client should ask how a repair is handled under the plan, as those that contain Freon® can’t be repaired. Of course, homeowners can’t be required to buy appliances mandated by the act in order to qualify for a home warranty plan.

## Clean Water Act (CWA)

The **Clean Water Act** (CWA) regulates discharges of pollutants such as oil and other hazardous substances into U.S. waters. It also establishes quality standards for surface waters. The CWA makes it illegal to dump pollutants from a point source, such as pipes and ditches, into navigable waters without a permit.

**How This May Affect Licensees**

This is a particular concern if a property contains or is located near wetlands, streams, lakes, rivers, ponds, or other protected waters. The Clean Water Act can significantly affect a property's use, development potential, value, and required permits. Licensees should recognize potential water-resource issues and encourage appropriate environmental due diligence.

## Resource Conservation and Recovery Act (RCRA)

The **Resource Conservation and Recovery Act** (RCRA) provides authority for the EPA to control all phases of hazardous waste: generation, transportation, storage, treatment, and disposal. Amendments to RCRA in 1986 extended the EPA’s authority to include underground storage tanks that hold petroleum and other hazardous substances.

**How This May Affect Licensees**

The RCRA can affect property value, marketability, financing, redevelopment potential, and environmental due diligence whenever hazardous waste issues are present. Licensees should recognize potential environmental concerns and encourage clients to obtain appropriate expert evaluations. Of particular concern are properties that were formerly used as:

- Gas stations
- Dry cleaners
- Manufacturing facilities
- Auto repair shops
- Agricultural chemical storage sites
- Industrial warehouses

## Toxic Substances Controls Act (TSCA)

The **Toxic Substances Control Act** (TSCA) regulates chemical substances that pose an unreasonable risk of injury to health and the environment. The TSCA gives the EPA authority to regulate the manufacture, processing, distribution, and use of chemical substances to protect human health and the environment

**How This May Affect Licensees**

The TSCA primarily affects real estate licensees through disclosure and risk-management obligations involving hazardous substances, especially lead-based paint in pre-1978 housing. Licensees should recognize potential toxic substance issues, ensure required disclosures are made, and encourage clients to obtain qualified environmental evaluations when appropriate.
