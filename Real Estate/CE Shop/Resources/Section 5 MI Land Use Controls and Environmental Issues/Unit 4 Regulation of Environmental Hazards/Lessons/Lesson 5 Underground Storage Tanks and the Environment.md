
## Underground Storage Tanks and the Environment

An Underground Storage Tank (UST) is a tank, along with its connected underground piping, where at least 10% of the total volume is located below ground. These tanks are commonly used to store petroleum products or hazardous chemicals. Due to the environmental risks of leaks and spills, USTs are regulated by the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) and may also be subject to state and local regulations.

In real estate transactions, the presence of a UST can have major implications. Leaks from these tanks can contaminate soil and groundwater, leading to costly cleanup efforts and legal liabilities for property owners. Buyers and sellers should be aware of these risks and ensure proper disclosure when a UST is present.

For example, a client looking at a former industrial site may need to investigate potential chemical contamination. In residential transactions, older homes may still have buried oil tanks used for heating, though modern systems typically place them above ground or use alternative fuel sources. However, many underground storage tanks still exist, making it essential for real estate professionals to identify potential hazards and ensure compliance with disclosure laws.

## Locating an Underground Storage Tank

During a home inspection, if a soil sample is taken near an underground soil tank and the soil is contaminated, the **seller** is required to clean up the soil and decommission the tank.

Let’s say a home has natural gas heating, but you don’t know if an oil tank were ever on the property. What signs should you look for? There may be a crimped-off, rusty oil line pipe in the basement or a plate covering an oil fill area outside the home.

If there’s a suspicion that a tank is or was on the property, check public records to see if a record of decommissioning can be found. If no record is found, a tank search should be conducted.

If a tank is found, the soil should be tested, because the owner of the property at the time contamination is found is responsible for decommissioning and clean-up. Tanks not in use and tanks in use that have leaked must be decommissioned properly.
