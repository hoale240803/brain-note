## Protected Classes in Michigan

In addition to the seven federally protected classes (race, color, religion, disability, sex, familial status, and national origin), Michigan fair housing laws add the protected classes of marital status and age. Keep in mind that local jurisdictions may add even more protected classes, and there may be additional protected classes that apply only to certain types of discrimination. For instance, age is a protected class at the federal level for _employment_—it is illegal to discriminate against someone for employment based on their age— but it is _not_ a protected class at the federal level for housing.

Don’t worry, we’ve sorted all of this out for you and included a list of protected classes that apply specifically to housing discrimination in your resource, but be sure to know your local fair housing laws, as well.

## Protection for Families

What do families with children and individuals with disabilities have in common? Until 1989, it was legal to discriminate against these (now) protected classes. Title VIII was amended in 1988 (effective March 12, 1989) by the Fair Housing Amendments Act, which added individuals with disabilities and families with children (and others covered under “familial status”) as federally protected classes. Until then, landlords could refuse to rent to a pregnant woman, restrict families with children to downstairs units in an apartment building, or even evict a female renter who became pregnant.

Your resource will cover what is meant by “familial status,” the term given to this relatively recent protected class, and what constitutes illegal discrimination.
