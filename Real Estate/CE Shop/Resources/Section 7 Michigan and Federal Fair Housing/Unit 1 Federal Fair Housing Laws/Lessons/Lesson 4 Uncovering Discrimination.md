## How Paired Testing Works

Housing discrimination contributes to broader social inequality. The U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) has relied on the use of paired testers when investigating discrimination.

In paired testing, the testers will have different attributes based on their membership in a protected class. For example, one tester will be Black and the other will be white. The testers will approach the same housing provider with a similar need, such as wanting to look at a listing, and they will present similar qualifications.

Paired testers can identify patterns of disparate treatment that exemplify discrimination, such as Black prospective buyers being shown fewer or worse listings than comparable white buyers.

Tester and test coordinator Justin Carter said about his work, “I’ve been involved on tests where an agent, you know, said very objectionable things, and said them again and again and again. […] You don’t want to encourage it, you don’t want to discourage it, but you just sort of have to take it.”

Catching bigots in their candid moments is uncomfortable work, but testers have been effective in revealing housing discrimination.
