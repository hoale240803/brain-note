
## Fair Housing Enforcement

Laws only work if there is a reason to follow them. Sure, most of us will do what’s right regardless. But without enforcement of the laws, there are some who would not. The tooth of any law is its enforcing agency and possible repercussions for violators.

## Filing a Fair Housing Complaint

The Office of Fair Housing and Equal Opportunity (FHEO) is part of the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD). The FHEO administrates and enforces federal fair housing laws; it also establishes policies to ensure equal access to housing choice and opportunity.

Members of a protected class (race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status, or disability) who believe they have been victims of housing discrimination may file fair housing complaints online, directly from HUD’s site. A complaint must be filed within one year from the time the suspected discrimination occurred. When a complaint is received, a fair housing specialist reviews it to determine whether the complaint involves a potential violation of the Fair Housing Act. The specialist contacts the consumer for additional information to complete this determination and, if the specialist decides that the Fair Housing Act may have been violated, the specialist helps the consumer file an official housing discrimination complaint.

The online complaint form is fairly straightforward and asks:

- **What happened?** The consumer must provide a description of the alleged discrimination.
- **Why do you believe discrimination occurred?** The consumer must cite his protected class status.
- **Who do you believe discriminated against you?** This must include the contact information.
- **Where did the discrimination occur?** This information should include address, city, state, and zip code.
- **When did the discrimination last occur?** The consumer is also asked whether the discrimination is continuous or ongoing.
