
## Federal Fair Housing Exemptions

As important as fair housing is, it was never intended to limit personal freedom or to create awkward housing situations and allows for some specific exemptions.

Fair housing law was designed to protect citizens from discrimination. It was not designed to prohibit non-discriminatory behavior, or even certain behaviors that discriminate, but to protect citizens seeking housing.

## Exemption Examples

Some exemptions to coverage under the Fair Housing Act include:

- **Owner-occupied dwellings with no more than four families living independently of each other**: Suppose an older widow were to rent out rooms in her home to help make ends meet. This imagined "Mrs. Murphy" is exempt from fair housing laws. This is referred to as the "Mrs. Murphy exemption." However, Mrs. Murphy could not run discriminatory advertising if she were to advertise her rental. And if Mrs. Murphy used the services of a real estate agent, the exemption would not apply.
    
- **Single-family housing sold or rented without the use of a broker:** If the private individual owner does not own more than three such single-family homes at one time.
    
- **Housing operated by religious organizations and private clubs that limit occupancy to members:** Religious organizations are allowed to give preference to and/or restrict occupancy in non-commercial housing to individuals of the same religion, as long as the religion doesn't restrict membership based on race, color, or national origin. Private clubs are also allowed to give preference to and/or restrict occupancy in non-commercial housing to members.
    
- **Housing designated for senior use:** Federal law allows an exemption _**on the basis of familial status only**_ for senior housing (not all protected classes). Senior housing must meet specific requirements to qualify for this exemption.
## Exemptions and Exceptions to the Rules

There are a handful of exemptions to federal fair housing laws, and these exemptions are equally as important as what is included in the law. For example, let’s say Mrs. Murphy is planning to rent out a room in her home. She’s very concerned about the idea of a man in her home, and she thinks a family will be too boisterous, so she wants to rent only to a single young woman. Technically, she's allowed to screen people accordingly. But even in this scenario, she can cross the line into violating fair housing laws. Which two of these actions would violate fair housing laws?

==🟢She puts an ad on Craigslist that specifies “young, single woman only.”==

She asks her friends if they know of anyone they can recommend.

==🟢She hires a real estate agent to help find a single, white tenant.==

## Mrs. Murphy Discriminates; Why Can’t Carly?

Carly, the property manager for several large apartment complexes, would like to control her tenant base just like Mrs. Murphy does. Why is Mrs. Murphy allowed to discriminate, when Carly isn't?

==🟢Mrs. Murphy lives in the property, which has four or fewer rental units.==

Mrs. Murphy is a widow, so no one else lives with her.

Mrs. Murphy is allowed to discriminate because of her age.

Mrs. Murphy is clear about the type of person she's looking for.

## Real Estate Professionals and the Mrs. Murphy Exemption

As a licensed professional, you're held to a higher standard. Whenever you get involved, fair housing laws will apply. You're never allowed to violate them, even if you’re helping Mrs. Murphy, and Mrs. Murphy meets all of the exemption requirements.

The takeaway here is that although this exemption does exist according to the law, it will never apply to you as a real estate professional. You’ll need to learn how to avoid discrimination, no matter the circumstance.

## Senior Housing Exemptions

You’re a licensee representing a client who wants to buy a condo. She’d like to buy a unit in a small complex near her work. You discover that the complex does have an available unit, and you contact the listing broker. You ask if you can show your client the unit, but the broker refuses, saying he can't sell to your client. Under what two circumstances would this be allowed under fair housing law?

The owner is over 62 years of age.

==🟢The property is owned by a religious organization or a private club.==

The units are designed for larger families.

The current residents are all male.

==🟢Residency in the condo complex is restricted to members of the organization that owns the complex.==
