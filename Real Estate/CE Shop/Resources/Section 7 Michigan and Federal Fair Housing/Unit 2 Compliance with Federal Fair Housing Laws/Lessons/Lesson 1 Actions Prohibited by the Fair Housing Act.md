
## Actions Prohibited by the Fair Housing Act

We would like to take some time to discuss what the federal Fair Housing Act prohibits. As a resource, we’ve provided a copy of a U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) poster about fair housing.

As a real estate licensee, you may not make housing unavailable to a person who is a member of a protected class. As a reminder, the Fair Housing Act prohibits housing discrimination on the basis of race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status, or disability.

You may not refuse to rent or sell housing to someone because they belong to a protected class. You also may not refuse to negotiate with such a person.

The Fair Housing Act also prohibits disparate treatment and misrepresentation when dealing with potential buyers and renters. You may not set different terms or conditions for a real estate transaction based on whether a participant belongs to a protected class. You also may not exclude protected class members from housing by saying it’s unavailable when it actually is available.

A good way to avoid trouble is to tell potential clients up front that you always follow fair housing laws.
