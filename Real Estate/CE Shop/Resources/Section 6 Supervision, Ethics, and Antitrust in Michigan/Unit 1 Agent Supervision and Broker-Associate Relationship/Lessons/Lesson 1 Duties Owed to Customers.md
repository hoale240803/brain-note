## What Duties Do You Owe Customers?

Let’s park the OLD CAR of client duties (obedience, loyalty, disclosure, confidentiality, accounting, and reasonable skill and care) for a moment and focus on the duties you owe to customers/third parties under real estate law. As a licensee, you **represent** clients and **assist** customers. This means that the level of service and duties you owe clients differs from the level of service and duties you owe customers.

Knowing which services and duties you owe to the client versus those you owe to the customer will prevent you from treating a customer as a client, which, of course, is something you want to avoid because it can create implied agency.


## Licensee’s Duties to Clients and Customers

Take a few minutes to review the Licensee’s Duties to Clients and Customers chart in your resources. You’ll notice that the licensee’s duties to the customer are also duties the licensee owes to the client. The “shared” duties are:

- **D**isclosure of material facts
- **R**easonable skill and care in performance
- **H**onesty and fair dealing
- **P**rompt presentation of all written offers and counter-offers

It’s certainly no OLD CAR but perhaps one way to remember the duties to customers is by thinking that old cars tend to drip (DRHP) oil. Pretty slick, eh?

## Duties Owed to Customers Recap

By now, there should be no question that, as a licensee, you owe duties and a certain level of service to both your clients and to customers, with the primary distinction being that you **represent** clients and **assist** customers.

These are duties that you owe to customers/third parties:

- **D**isclosure of material facts
- **R**easonable skill and care
- **H**onesty and fair dealing
- **P**rompt presentation of all written offers and counter-offers

Just remember the oil drip (DRHP) from that old car, and you’ll be fine.
