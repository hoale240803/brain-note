## The Broker-Salesperson Relationship

One of the most important relationships you can have in your real estate career is with your broker. Choose your broker wisely. Your broker can be your mentor, advocate, source of leads, trainer, and supervisor. A great broker can get your career off to a great start. And great personal assistants are worth their weight in gold.

**The Broker-Salesperson Relationship**

As a brand new real estate licensee, you can’t just go out on your own and begin selling real estate. You must place your license under that of an experienced broker. Brokers have the expertise and the supervisory responsibilities to assist you early in your real estate career.

Brokers are responsible for the actions of their associated licensees, as well as other members of the firm. You should be able to look to your broker for guidance and assistance with getting started in your career, although developing business will be your responsibility. You are required to take listings only in your broker’s name and advertise your services only through your broker/brokerage. You are also required to have your broker review all transactional paperwork and advertising.

The terms of your broker/salesperson relationship are spelled out in your independent contractor agreement (or employment agreement if you are an employee). You should also receive a copy of your firm’s policies and procedures.

If you have any questions while in the practice of real estate, your broker should be your first phone call. Not only is it the broker’s responsibility, her license is also on the line.

## Hiring a Personal Assistant

At some point in your career, you may desire the services of a personal assistant. Your broker may actually provide you with one as part of your package. Whenever you hire a personal assistant, both you and your broker are responsible for that person’s activities.

The most important consideration when working with a personal assistant is whether you will hire a licensed or unlicensed assistant. A licensed assistant may legally perform the same brokerage services you are allowed to perform, and is subject to the same licensing laws. An unlicensed assistant may not do any activities for which a license is required. These include negotiating on behalf of clients, completing real estate forms, or discussing terms with clients and prospects. If an unlicensed assistant performs activities requiring a license, you can lose your license, and your broker’s license is also in jeopardy.
