
## We Can't Make This Stuff Up

What follows is a true story. We promise.

At a real estate seminar in a breakout session about referrals, the facilitator of the session asked, “What do each of you do in your business to gain referrals?” A real estate licensee who has been in the business for several years, announced, “I just tell all my friends and family that if they refer someone to me, I’ll give them $200.”

The other occupants in the room gasped. When the facilitator recovered his composure, he told the licensee making the remark, “Unfortunately, that's completely illegal.”

Compensating unlicensed individuals for activities requiring a license is a license law violation.

## Compensation Comes Through the Broker

If you’re ever worried about whether you should accept or give a fee for a referral, perhaps you can find some peace of mind from the following: All compensation related to real estate must come through your broker.

You are not to receive any compensation directly from a seller, buyer, other agents, other brokers, or your Aunt Bea without it going through your broker. (Yes, your Aunt Bea can send you a birthday card with $10 in it, but she may not pay you for real estate services directly. All such compensation must be funneled through your broker.) Since your broker will undoubtedly have a better handle on what constitutes a legal referral fee, following this one simple guideline should help keep you out of trouble.

## No Undisclosed Compensation

Here’s another guideline that will help: **You may not receive undisclosed compensation from any party related to the transaction**.

That means everyone has to know who’s paying you and why. So if you were representing the buyer, and the seller’s broker offered to pay your broker $5,000 for producing an acceptable offer no later than a particular date you need to disclose that bonus to your buyer. The reason is simple: The incentive creates an inherent conflict of interest.

## Settlement Service Providers

The Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act (RESPA) prohibits you from receiving or paying referral fees or finder fees from/to mortgage brokers, title reps, or any other settlement service provider.

## What's Permitted?

Your broker can provide referral fees to and from other licensees within your firm for referring you business or pay referral fees to another broker for business sent by their licensees (e.g., your cousin is a real estate licensee in another state and refers his former college roommate to you, and you pay your cousin a referral fee).

**Referral fees to and from other real estate licensees are perfectly legal—however, they must be disclosed, and such referral fee payments must go through the brokers.**
