
Should it Stay or Should it Go?

Sometimes, it’s hard to tell whether something's real property or personal property, but it's important to know the difference between them. Why? Real property stays, while personal property goes (unless the buyers and sellers negotiate otherwise, which should be captured by the sales contract). When real property stays, we say it conveys with the property.

Let's review how to distinguish personal property (which may also be called chattel or personalty) from real property by looking at some examples on the next slide.

## Real Property or Personal Property

Did you notice trends in the differences between the two types of property? If you can use the following words to describe property, it's most likely considered **real property**:

- Custom
- Built-in
- Attached
- Bracketed
- In-ground
- Fixture

In other words, if it's affixed to the property or has otherwise been customized to fit it specifically, it's more likely to be treated as real property that would be conveyed with a sale.

**Personal property**, on the other hand, is generally property that's:

- Easily moveable
- Clearly not related to the property (i.e., furniture, clothing)
- Free-standing
- Of a personal nature (e.g., photos, wall decor)

## Two Common Exceptions

Did you find it challenging to differentiate real property from personal property (personalty)? A lot of people do. Because it can be confusing, it’s best to negotiate (and get in writing) the disposition of any questionable items.

However, two common categories of property can become particularly confusing in the real property vs. personal property debate.

**Commercial**

For commercial properties, sometimes affixed items are considered **trade fixtures** and while they may be attached to real property, they are treated as personal property. Trade fixtures are equipment placed on or attached to real estate that the renter uses in their trade or business. An example would be display shelves installed by a business owner in a rented building. Provided they are removed before lease expiration, trade fixtures generally may be taken by the tenant.

**Agriculture**

For agricultural properties, **emblements** are crops produced through cultivation belonging to the tenant during and after harvest. Like trade fixtures, even though these are attached to the land at some point, they're still considered the legal, personal property of the tenant.

## Avoiding Confusion

![](https://ces-prd-assets.leaponline.com/images/c574011f-f493-40fe-abee-d0c0730faf75_tip.jpeg)When in doubt, specify in the listing agreement or sales agreement the items that the seller wishes to remove upon selling the property, or the items that the buyer wishes to remain with the property. Aside from the structure itself, what's "real" or "personal" may be subject to interpretation—so it's best to be crystal clear in writing.
