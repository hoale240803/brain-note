## Describing Land

In contrast to a mere street address, a legal description is a description of the land that specifies the boundaries and location of a piece of real property. Legal descriptions appear on deeds and other legal documents because the description is sufficient to adequately describe and define a property so the courts can correctly determine ownership boundaries.

Here’s an example of a street address:

123 Main Street, Anytown USA, State 55555

Here might be the same property as defined in a legal description:

**COMMENCING** at the intersection of northern 50’ Right of Way of Main Street and Eastern 40’ Right of Way of North Avenue. THENCE leaving said point along the northern Right of Way of North Street 90 degrees 00 minutes 00 seconds East a distance of 175.00 feet to ½” Rebar Found on the northern 50’ Right of Way of Main Street, said ½” Rebar Found being the **TRUE POINT OF BEGINNING**.

THENCE from said **TRUE POINT OF BEGINNING** following said right of way North 90 degrees 00 minutes 00 seconds East a distance of 75.00 feet to ½” Rebar Found. THENCE leaving said right of way North 00 degrees 00 minutes 00 seconds West a distance of 150.00 feet to ½’ Rebar Set. THENCE South 90 degrees 00 minutes 00 seconds West a distance of 75.00 feet to 1” Crimp Top Pipe Found. THENCE South 00 degrees 00 minutes 00 seconds East a distance of 150.00 feet to ½” Rebar Found, said ½” Rebar Found on the northern 50’ Right of Way of Main Street being the **TRUE POINT OF BEGINNING**.

Sometimes, a survey will be required to verify, clarify, correct, or create a legal description. A survey is a professional on-site measurement of a property's lot lines and dimensions, including the location of any improvements (such as a house) on the lot, any encroachments that may exist, and any easements of public record.
