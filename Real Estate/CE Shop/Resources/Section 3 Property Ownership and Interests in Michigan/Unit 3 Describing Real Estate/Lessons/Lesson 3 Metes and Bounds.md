## Metes and Bounds

Let's take a closer look at the ==🟡metes and bounds== system, one of the oldest land measurement systems used in the U.S.

Metes and bounds property descriptions are frequently found in deeds. But what are metes? And what is a bound?

- **Metes** refer to the direction and distance of a line that forms the property boundary. A compass showing north, south, east, and west and the degrees of direction between zero and 90 determines the direction.
- **Bounds** are the physical features that define the boundaries of the land. Common terms used in bounds could include the names of trees, creeks, neighboring landowners, or even piles of rocks.

The language and numbers used in a metes and bounds description are derived from a survey performed by a licensed land surveyor. A key consideration is the point of beginning. Unless it is easily identifiable, the rest of the description won’t be of much use to anyone, because everything is based off of that point. After identifying this point, the surveyor sights the direction to the next point.

Example

Beginning at a stake and stones about 30 feet from the center of the brook that runs across the road south westerly from the dwelling house of the late Mr. Jebediah Smith now occupied by Ms. Callie Thornton and in the west line of the highway leading by the dwelling house now occupied by the Ms. Callie Thornton…

## Point of Beginning

In a metes and bounds description, the point of beginning (POB) is extremely important. What's the importance of the point of beginning?

==🟢The entire description is based off of the point of beginning.==

The point of beginning is where the entrance to the property will be located.

It provides the directional description for the property boundary.
